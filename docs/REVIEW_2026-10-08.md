# Critical review and rebuild of the CIF code base, 2026-10-07/08

This note records what was reviewed, what was wrong, what was changed, and what the rebuilt tool shows.  Evidence for
every claim is in the run records under `runs/`, in the tests, and in the reviewers' reports summarised in the table
below (the reports themselves are working notes, not part of the archive).

## 1. What was asked

The code must reproduce the papers' devices from the **decoupled cutoff D and filtrate fraction F** (plus the other
parameters the papers give), never by computing an internal parameter by hand; every part (layout, taper, meander yes/no
and style, inlet, outlet, chip area) must be a composable choice; every resulting device must be validated physically to
realise the user's D and F; and the output must be a photomask with multipanel renders that show the device is right.

## 3. Answers to the specific questions

* **Is Dinh's predicted F (11 % high) the preprint's finding?**  Yes, and by construction: both evaluate the outlet
  identity at Dinh's printed outlet widths (F_closed 0.5592 against the measured 0.505 ± 0.033).  The preprint attributes
  the gap to 3-D outlet bifurcation (COMSOL 0.5070); unstated outlet tubing of 6.5–9.7 cm fits both variants within 1σ as
  well, so the attribution is not unique.  The preprint's N (1407 / 1642) is not printed in any paper.
* **Is the cutoff validated even where the tool refuses the device?**  Yes: strict mode refuses and still reports the cutoff
  band; evaluate and reproduce modes build and measure.  The published measurements do not test D = κ_g δ* sharply:
  the glossary finds Dinh's bracket a weak consistency check and Xia's agreement not independent, and Gifford's
  held-out Fig. 5 devices report cumulative retention in long devices, while κ_g was fitted on an onset-of-loss
  threshold in short arrays.  At D the Fig. 5 devices retain R(D) = 0.39–0.42, 0.44–0.54, 0.38–0.48 and 0.17–0.30 of
  the beads (d₅₀/D = 1.09–1.14, 0.98–1.03, 1.01–1.04, ≥ 1.10; three interpolants × three enrichment ceilings,
  `provenance/gifford_fig5_d50.py`).  That is a statement about loss in a long device, not a test of κ_g at ±3.4 %
  (revised 2026-10-10: the earlier wording, "no measured separation contradicts D = κ_g δ*" and "5B within 2 % of D",
  treated the d₅₀ as such a test).  The plateau edge
  u = 0.035 is a bin edge in an empty interval of Gifford Fig. 3's points; below it the record gives a band.
* **Why did the old geometry check crash on Gifford, and does it work now?**  CIF_v2's J8/J10 rebuilt a straight one-leg
  mask instead of measuring the given one, and the engine refused that rebuild (171 rows narrower than the meander
  channel).  A patch (CIF_v2 copy only; v2.1.0 stays frozen) makes them measure the given mask: 7/7 on Gifford.  The new
  tool measures every drawn mask by construction.
* **Are the meander designs physics-adherent?**  See §5: every head style is built inside the variant matrix and checked
  gap by gap on the drawn mask.

## 6. What remains

* Composition: parallel arrays with per-element outlets (implemented, `opencif/compose.py`; only clean PASS elements);
  merged outlets, series stage sequences and cascades are not implemented.
* Tapers: constant and constant-shear only (no linear or user profile).
* Dinh's filter is in absolute coordinates (fits only his head); the ring outlet is parametric but tested on few cases.
* The realised cutoff is the 1-D network on the drawn geometry, with the exact Stokes correction at bends; 3-D effects at
  gap mouths, at the straight–arc junctions and at the outlet bifurcation are future work (docs/LIMITATIONS.md).
* Still design values: the post length across the flow (l_g) and the side channels' inner edge, the radii of head and
  outlet arcs (widths measured), head rows' centre-path lengths, off-chip tubing.
* Head capacity planning uses geometric lengths; with a negative bend term (shallow channels) a unit can draw longer
  than planned (R3 minor 7, inferred, not exercised by any preset).
* The seam rows of a meander head are intrinsically sensitive: a few tenths of a percent of the head's resistance move
  them by percent.  The junction band shows where the 1-D model cannot pin them down; fabrication tolerance will move
  them as well.
* `tests/test_appendix_trace.py` pins the App. A results, App. B claims and glossary macros the code relies on (68
  checks at rc3; `docs/ALIGNMENT.md` maps each).  It does not audit the dissertation text.
* Seal versus size: a sealed packing head is compact but, at small cutoffs, long (Dinh's targets: 4.2 mm wide and
  ~170 mm in all, against ~150–154 mm for Dinh's unsealed V1 as reproduced here; the paper prints 75.8 mm, which the
  preset notes find in conflict with the premised 1-D model at the presets' F, and at the measured F too for a centre
  channel narrowed progressively, as Dinh describes it, at the measured pitch (PRESET_PROVENANCE; row 66)).  The papers' heads are drawn unsealed only with gap 1 declared.
* Speed: 1–30 min per build.
